\subsection{Five-chunk error under observed-motion conditions}
We extend the horizon to five consecutive chunks on the preregistered Bridge
record 15, using the first 81 real observations and 80 aligned transitions.
Two noise schedules, $(0,1,2,3,4)$ and $(5,6,7,8,9)$, are paired between
teacher-forced (TF) and autoregressive (AR) predictions at each horizon.
TF uses the current real frame; AR uses its own preceding floating-point
endpoint through the verified encoding interface. The initial chunk is shared.
All 20 formal calls and the two exact-repeat gates passed. These are two
noise schedules on one episode, not independent scene replicates.

Values are descriptive means of the two schedules; $\Delta_h$ is the paired
AR-minus-TF RGB RMSE difference. Both schedules have positive differences at
$h=2,3,5$ and negative differences at $h=4$. All eight condition-latent RMS
differences are positive. Thus the choice of output space changes the comparison.
The AR RGB error is nonmonotone, including a decrease from chunk 4 to 5.
This does not contradict a Lipschitz upper bound: such a bound is not an
observed growth law.

For aligned vectors $b=f_{\rm TF}-x^\star$ and
$p=f_{\rm AR}-f_{\rm TF}$ in either separately defined output space,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{MSE}_{\rm AR}-\operatorname{MSE}_{\rm TF}
 =\frac{2\langle b,p\rangle}{n}+\frac{\|p\|_2^2}{n}.
 \label{eq:task11-geometry}
\end{equation}
This follows by expanding $\|b+p\|_2^2$ and requires no differentiability.
At chunk 4 in RGB space, the negative cross term outweighs the nonnegative
feedback-change term. When the combined RMSE is nonzero, division of
\eqref{eq:task11-geometry} by
$\operatorname{RMSE}_{\rm AR}+\operatorname{RMSE}_{\rm TF}$ gives
the RMSE difference. This explains how nonzero feedback displacement can
reduce target-relative error, without identifying separate model and numerical
causes. The action conditions include future observed poses; this is not
control-only open-loop prediction. One episode and five chunks cannot establish
population or long-term stability.

\subsection{Actual prediction-error spectra and episode holdout}
Using the six previously fixed episodes and two noise schedules per episode,
we form separate 12-column matrices for $E_1$, $E_{{\rm TF},2}$ and
$E_{{\rm AR},2}$. Columns are predictions minus aligned truth, not perturbation
responses. In condition-latent space, all three uncentered matrices require
$k_{95}=10$ of 12 modes. Their squared-energy entropy effective ranks are
$9.2678$, $9.1630$ and $9.5396$.

For each leave-one-episode-out fold, both seeds of the omitted episode are
held out and the other ten columns determine the basis. Even retaining the
entire training span, mean relative held-out residual norms are respectively
$0.96569$, $0.96982$ and $0.96681$, averaging seeds within each episode first.
These are residual norms, not residual energy fractions. The results do not
support a very small transferable linear error subspace for this finite sample.
Rank at most 12 is imposed by construction, not evidence of intrinsic low rank.
Neither the full Jacobian nor nonlinear or spatially aligned representations
are characterized by this experiment.
